o note, some of these papers can get quite complex especially in the sections where they discuss their methodology. If you are finding a paper difficult to read, we suggest that you focus on the introduction section and understanding the problem the researchers were looking to solve, as well as their results and conclusion.  

1. Research paper \- Ashish Vaswani, Noam Shazeer, Niki Parmar, Jakob Uszkoreit, Llion Jones, Aidan N. Gomez, Łukasz Kaiser, and Illia Polosukhin \- [Attention is All You Need](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2017/file/3f5ee243547dee91fbd053c1c4a845aa-Paper.pdf) 

   This is a seminal paper that introduced the original transformer architecture and the self-attention mechanism \[1\].

2. Research paper \- Dzmitry Bahdanau, Kyunghyun Cho, and Yoshua Bengio \- [Neural Machine Translation by Jointly Learning to Align and Translate](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0473)

   This paper introduced the original attention mechanism. Originally it had been exclusively used for machine translation with the idea that token representations in the target language should attend to token representations in the source language \[2\].  
3. Research paper \- Thang Luong, Hieu Pham, and Christopher D. Manning \- [Effective Approaches to Attention-based Neural Machine Translation](https://aclanthology.org/D15-1166/)

   This paper introduced the dot product attention mechanism that inspired the self-attention mechanism for transformers \[3\].  
4. Research paper \- Jianlin Su, Murtadha Ahmed, Yu Lu, Shengfeng Pan, Wen Bo, and Yunfeng Liu \- [RoFormer: Enhanced transformer with Rotary Position Embedding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09864) 

   This paper introduced the rotary positional embeddings, which are used in many transformer-based models for encoding positional information about the tokens \[4\].  
5. Research paper – Emily Dawson, Stephen Hughes, Simon Lock and Michel Wahome \- [Exploring the politics of science communication research: looking at science communication from a social justice perspective](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10158494/)

   This paper argues that mainstream science communication research has often reinforced inequalities by privileging dominant groups and Western perspectives. It calls for reimagining the field through social justice, inclusion, and valuing diverse knowledge, practices, and emotions \[5\].  
6. Article – Jack Stilgoe \- [AI has a democracy problem. Citizens assemblies can help.](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adr6713)

   This article argues that decisions about AI such as ethical boundaries and governance should involve democratic processes like citizens’ assemblies to ensure legitimacy, accountability, and public trust, rather than being controlled solely by corporations or regulators. The paper underscores the value of community participation in shaping AI \[6\].
